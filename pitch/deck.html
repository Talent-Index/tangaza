The problem

Word of mouth builds Kenyan businesses.
Nobody can pay for it safely.

  • The advocates get nothing. The customer who brings five friends gets a handshake.
  • Loyalty points die of success. Unlimited emission → unbudgeted liability → program cancelled, trust burned.
  • Influencer spend is unverifiable. You pay for reach, not for people through the door.

One contract fixes both sides: advocacy becomes attested and payable, and the budget becomes physically impossible to overrun.

One behaviour · one mechanic

The loop

1

Advocate’s wallet writes the activity on-chain — submitActivity(), gas sponsored, msg.sender is the identity

2

Business approves with its wallet — the contract rejects every other signer (NotApprover)

3

Every 20 approved activities mints a KES 500 credit — engagement weights set by the business price each act

4

Burn-on-redeem — airtime, data or voucher; every redemption shrinks the business’s liability

Composed from the cohort’s building blocks — capped token + streaks + tiers — in one contract, 45 tests passing.

Can it survive success?

The chain enforces the CFO

1 · Budget-backed

No org exists without committing emissionCapKES. Every credit is KES 500 of airtime, data or voucher.

2 · Capped emission

Written once. No function can raise it — CI pins the contract’s full function list; verified source on Snowtrace.

3 · Sinks that work

Burn-on-redeem shrinks outstanding liability — and redemptions never reopen minting headroom.

4 · Value from the product

Earned by attested behaviour, spent on real rewards. Non-transferable: no market, no speculation loop.

At the cap: activities still record, BudgetExhausted fires, nothing reverts — worst-case cost is known on day one.
