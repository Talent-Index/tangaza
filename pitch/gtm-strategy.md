# Ubu-Tangaza — Go-to-Market & Customer/User Strategy

*Working doc, Aug 2026. Owner: Dan. Grounded in the incubation validation playbook
(Retail & SME Loyalty bounty) and the product as shipped at ubutangaza.biz.*

---

## 1. Positioning — the one sentence

> **Ubu-Tangaza lets a local business see, track, and reward the customers who bring
> it new customers — with proof for every action and zero dashboard babysitting.**

We are NOT selling "web3 rewards" or "a loyalty platform." We're selling the answer to
one question every shop owner already asks: *"who actually brings me customers, and
how do I keep them doing it?"* The chain is plumbing, never pitch.

## 2. The two-sided rule that shapes everything

**Deals drive users.** Advocates (users) arrive *through* businesses (customers), not
before them. A shop signs up → its regulars become advocates → their friends become
the shop's new customers → some of those become advocates. So the entire GTM points at
merchants first; there is no separate consumer-acquisition motion until Phase 3.

## 3. Beachhead customer (ICP)

| Dimension | Beachhead |
|---|---|
| Who | Owner-operated shops: coffee/tea spots, salons & barbershops, boutiques, gyms/studios |
| Where | One Nairobi neighborhood at a time (density matters — see §6) |
| Size | 1–15 staff, owner makes decisions same-day |
| Payment | M-Pesa Till on the counter |
| Existing marketing | WhatsApp status, Instagram, word of mouth. No CRM, no loyalty software |
| Pain trigger | Says some version of "customers bring their friends" but can't name who |

Disqualifiers (for now): franchises, online-only stores, anyone needing procurement
or a committee to say yes.

## 4. Phased GTM

### Phase 0 — Validate (this week → +10 days). *The gate.*
- **5–8 discovery interviews** with ICP owners, using the accelerator's script:
  1. "How do you know if a new customer came because a friend recommended them?"
  2. "How much would you pay a loyal customer for bringing a new paying client?"
  3. "Why haven't you installed digital referral software on your counter?"
- Capture: willingness-to-pay per referral, preferred reward form (cash vs
  discount/product), why past tools failed, objections.
- Exit criteria: ≥5 owners confirm the attribution pain AND name a price they'd pay
  per referred customer. If they don't → revisit the wedge before building more.

### Phase 1 — Pilot (10 days, runs right after interviews)
- **Recruit 3 merchants** from the interviews (best energy, busiest counters).
- **Concierge onboarding** — do NOT expect self-serve: sit with the owner, register
  the shop, set 1–2 activities + rewards, print the QR, brief the staff, personally
  onboard their 5–10 most loyal regulars as the first advocates.
- **Target: 15 verified referred purchases across the 3 shops in 10 days.** That
  number is the PMF proof for the accelerator and for the next 20 merchants.
- Weekly WhatsApp check-in with each owner; fix friction same-day.

### Phase 2 — Densify (weeks 3–8)
- Expand to **20 merchants in the same neighborhood** before touching a second one.
  Density makes advocates multi-shop (one person earning at 3 shops = habit), makes
  word-of-mouth between owners do the selling, and makes support physically cheap.
- Convert the waitlist: every signup gets a call within 24h and a visit within a week.
- Publish pilot results as the sales asset: "Shop X got N referred sales in 10 days."

### Phase 3 — Repeatable motion (month 3+)
- Second neighborhood, playbook-driven; first hire = a field onboarder, not an engineer.
- Channel partners: M-Pesa agents, POS resellers, business WhatsApp groups, chamas.
- Only now: light advocate-side marketing ("earn from the shops you already love").

## 5. Customer (business) acquisition strategy

**Primary channel: physical walk-ins.** The counter IS the channel. Assets in hand:
- **/for-business** — QR + one-tap share; owner scans, lands on the pitch, joins on the spot.
- **Launch video** on the phone (30s) + **the article** as the WhatsApp follow-up.
- **/waitlist** — the pitch page; every field visit that doesn't close ends with it.

**The pitch (30 seconds, at the counter):**
> "Who brings you the most new customers? …You don't know, right? This shows you —
> with proof — and rewards them however you choose: airtime, a discount, a free
> anything. It runs itself; you'll never open a dashboard. First shops run it free.
> Scan this."

**Secondary channels:** the accelerator network (intros + credibility), Immaculate's
and your own first-degree owner contacts, the waitlist funnel from social posts of
the launch video/article.

**Offer:** pilot is free, forever-free tier likely (see §8). The business's only cost
is the rewards they choose to honor — which they control absolutely (fixed budget,
their own reward forms). "Nothing upfront, reward only verified actions" is the
whole objection-killer.

## 6. User (advocate) strategy

- **Source:** each merchant's existing regulars, onboarded at the counter during
  concierge setup ("bring your 10 best customers, we'll set them up in 5 minutes").
- **Activation:** signed in (Google — no wallet visible) + shared their link once.
  Target: ≥60% of onboarded regulars share within 48h.
- **The habit loop:** share → friend acts → proof lands → progress bar moves →
  reward at the threshold. Levels + per-activity goals ("2 more referrals to your
  free cut") are the retention machine — every merchant must have at least one
  achievable level (~1 week of normal effort) and one aspirational one.
- **Multi-shop flywheel (Phase 2):** density means the same advocate earns across
  shops; that's when the advocate side starts pulling merchants in ("my customers
  asked if we're on Ubu-Tangaza").
- **Trust promise to advocates:** rewards are honored — we track "owed vs delivered"
  per merchant and quietly drop merchants who don't honor. One stiffed advocate in a
  neighborhood kills the flywheel.

## 7. Metrics that matter (per phase)

| Phase | North star | Guardrails |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 5+ owners confirm pain + name a price | — |
| 1 | **15 verified referred purchases / 10 days** | time-to-onboard < 45 min; advocate share-rate ≥ 60%; zero owner dashboard-minutes required |
| 2 | 20 active merchants, 1 neighborhood | ≥50% merchants with a referred sale each week; rewards honored ≤ 7 days; waitlist→visit ≤ 7 days |
| 3 | Repeatable CAC per merchant | advocate 4-week retention; multi-shop advocates % |

## 8. Monetization (position now, charge later)

Free pilot. Do not price before Phase 2 — the interviews' willesness-to-pay data
decides. Candidate models, in order of fit:
1. **Per-verified-referral fee** (KES 20–50) — perfectly aligned: they pay only when
   we provably brought a customer. Matches the interview question the accelerator
   made us ask.
2. Flat monthly (KES 500–1,500) once a shop is habituated.
3. Never: a % of the reward (poisons trust), or charging advocates (kills supply).

## 9. What kills this (watch list)

1. **Friction at the moment of advocacy** — if sharing or proving takes >60 seconds
   on a phone, it dies. (Includes the 8-char hex referral code — shorten it.)
2. **Merchants not honoring rewards** — enforce the owed-list; drop offenders.
3. **Selling the tech instead of the outcome** — no owner buys "on-chain"; they buy
   "more customers, provably."
4. **Skipping density** — 20 shops in 20 neighborhoods is 20 pilots; 20 in one is a market.
5. **Building instead of visiting** — the product is ahead of the validation. Ship
   nothing new (except pilot-blockers) until Phase 1 runs.
