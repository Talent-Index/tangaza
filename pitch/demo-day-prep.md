## 1. The pitch in 30 seconds (say this first, before any slide)

"Every small business grows by word of mouth, and not one owner can tell you which
customers bring them new ones. Ubu-Tangaza makes that visible — with proof — and lets
the business reward it however it wants: airtime, a discount, a free coffee. It runs
itself, the reward budget can never be overrun, and it's live today at ubutangaza.biz.
We're onboarding our first three shops in one Nairobi neighbourhood this month."

## 2. Value proposition (one line + three proofs)

**For a local shop:** see exactly who brings you customers, back every action with proof,
reward it your way, with zero dashboard babysitting.

- **Visible** — a live, per-customer view of referrals, posts, visits: who did what.
- **Provable** — a photo, a post, a receipt on every claim; approvals attested on-chain.
- **Controlled** — you set what counts, what it earns, and a budget that can't be raised.

| Who | Owner-operated, walk-in shops: coffee/tea spots, salons & barbers, boutiques, gyms/studios |

## 4. Business model — "Why will they pay?"

- **Pilot is free.** Nothing upfront; the only cost is the rewards they choose to honour.
- **Primary model: per-verified-referral fee (KES 20–50).** They pay only when we
  provably brought them a paying customer. Perfectly aligned; the accelerator's own
  discovery question ("how much would you pay a customer for bringing a new client?")
  prices it for us — every interview is a pricing data point.
- **Later: flat monthly (KES 500–1,500)** once a shop is habituated.
- **Never:** a cut of the reward (poisons trust) or charging the advocate (kills supply).
- Unit economics at 20 shops × 30 verified referrals/mo × KES 30 ≈ KES 18k MRR, near-zero
  marginal cost (serverless + testnet-cost chain writes). Path to KES 1M ARR is ~10
  neighbourhoods.

## 5. Go-to-market

Rule that shapes everything: **deals drive users.** Advocates come THROUGH businesses.

1. **Validate (now):** 5–8 owner interviews with the accelerator script.
2. **Pilot (10 days):** 3 shops, concierge-onboarded. Target **15 verified referred
   purchases**.
3. **Densify:** 20 shops in ONE neighbourhood before a second one.
4. **Repeat:** second neighbourhood; first hire is a field onboarder, not an engineer.

## 6. Customer acquisition plan

- **Channel = the counter.** Walk in with the phone: `/for-business` shows a QR the
  owner scans → lands on the pitch → joins in a minute. Video on the phone, article as
  the WhatsApp follow-up, waitlist to close every visit that doesn't sign.
- **The 30-second counter pitch:** "Who brings you the most new customers? …You don't
  know, right? This shows you — with proof — and rewards them however you choose. It
  runs itself. First shops run it free. Scan this."
- **Warm channels:** this cohort network, our own first-degree owners, waitlist from the
  launch video/article. Every waitlist signup: call in 24h, visit in a week.
- **CAC target:** one field visit ≈ one signup at density; ~KES 300–500 per shop.

## 7. Distribution strategy

- **Advocates distribute the business** — every customer becomes a channel via their
  personal link and QR. That's the product's distribution loop, not ours.
- **Density → multi-shop advocates** — the same person earning at three shops on one
  street makes owners recruit each other ("my customers asked if we're on it").
- **Partners (Phase 3):** M-Pesa agents, POS resellers, business WhatsApp groups, chamas.
- **Platform surfaces we own:** ubutangaza.biz (waitlist, pitch), /for-business (QR),
  the launch video, the article.

## 8. Defensibility — "What makes this hard to replicate?"

Be honest: the code is not the moat. The moat is what the code accumulates.

1. **A verified referral graph, per neighbourhood.** Every approved action is proof-backed
   and attributed. After 20 shops we hold the only trustworthy map of who moves customers
   between businesses on that street. A copycat starts at zero and has to earn trust shop
   by shop.
2. **Density network effects.** Multi-shop advocates make each new shop more valuable to
   join and each advocate more valuable to keep. Whoever is dense first wins the block.
3. **Trust you can't fake.** The immutable budget and on-chain attestation are a promise
   a Web2 loyalty tool can't make: the business provably can't stiff advocates or overrun
   its costs. That's a sales line, and it compounds into reputation.
4. **Regulatory position.** No custody, no payouts through us — attribution only. We stay
   outside VASP scope and move faster than fintech-regulated competitors who must license
   before they can pilot.
5. **Execution moat:** concierge onboarding + field playbook in a market global tools
   don't serve (ReferralCandy/Friendbuy are e-commerce, USD, card-first).

## 10. The four questions — crisp answers

**Who are you building for?** Owner-operated walk-in shops in one Nairobi neighbourhood
— coffee, salons, boutiques — that grow by word of mouth and can't see it.

**Why will they pay?** Because we charge only when we provably brought them a paying
customer. No upfront cost, no subscription until it's a habit. They're already paying
for referrals — in free coffees and handshakes — invisibly and unfairly. We make it
measurable and cheaper.

**How will you reach them?** At the counter, in person, one neighbourhood at a time —
QR in hand. Then their customers reach the next shop for us.

**What's hard to replicate?** The verified referral graph and the density. Code is
copyable; a street where every shop and regular already trusts one reward network isn't.

## 11. Tough questions you'll get — and answers

- **"Why blockchain? Owners don't care."** Correct — and they never see it. It's there
  for one reason: an immutable budget and tamper-proof attestation are a trust promise
  we can make and a Web2 app can't. Cut it and we're another loyalty app asking to be
  trusted.
- **"Isn't this just a referral form?"** A form takes their word. We take proof, attach
  it to an approval nobody can rewrite, and count it against a budget nobody can raise.
- **"What about M-Pesa / VASP?"** We don't touch money. Attribution only; the business
  honours rewards its way. Deliberately outside VASP scope.
- **"How do you stop fake referrals?"** Proof on every claim, business approval, and
  the referrer is a real identity across shops — abuse costs reputation on the whole
  street.
- **"Traction?"** Honest: product live, first pilot this month, 15-verified-sales target.
  We're here to validate, not to claim.
- **"Why you?"** Built and shipped it in weeks, it's live, we're in the shops this week.
