The problem

Word of mouth builds every Kenyan shop.
No owner can see it — or reward it.

  • Ask a shop owner who brings them customers. "People tell their friends." Which people? Silence.
  • The regular who sent fifteen friends gets a handshake — so the behaviour never compounds.
  • Every loyalty tool became a second job. Another dashboard, another queue. Abandoned within a month.
  • Points programs die of success. Unbudgeted liability → cancelled → trust burned.

Make the referral visible, back it with proof, reward it the owner’s way, and make the budget physically impossible to overrun. Then get out of the way.

The loop

1

Advocate’s wallet writes the activity on-chain — submitActivity(), gas sponsored, msg.sender is the identity

2

Business approves with its wallet — the contract rejects every other signer (NotApprover)

3

Every 20 approved activities mints a reward credit — engagement weights set by the business price each act

4

Burn-on-redeem — the credit becomes an in-house offer, discount or voucher; every redemption shrinks the business’s liability

Composed from the cohort’s building blocks — capped token + streaks + tiers — in one contract, 45 tests passing.

Can it survive success?

The chain enforces the CFO

1 · Budget-backed

No org exists without committing its emissionCap. Every credit is a real in-house reward — an offer, a discount, a voucher — budgeted before it exists.

2 · Capped emission

Written once. No function can raise it — CI pins the contract’s full function list; verified source on Snowtrace.

3 · Sinks that work

Burn-on-redeem shrinks outstanding liability — and redemptions never reopen minting headroom.

4 · Value from the product

Earned by attested behaviour, spent on real rewards. Non-transferable: no market, no speculation loop.

At the cap: activities still record, BudgetExhausted fires, nothing reverts — worst-case cost is known on day one.

How we reach them

The counter is the channel.

1 · Validate — now

5–8 owner interviews with the accelerator script. Exit rule: five owners confirm the pain and name a price per referred customer.

2 · Pilot — 10 days

3 shops, concierge-onboarded at the counter; we set up their rewards and enrol their 10 best regulars ourselves. Target: 15 verified referred purchases.

3 · Densify, then repeat

20 shops in one neighbourhood before a second. First hire: a field onboarder, not an engineer.

The 30-second pitch, in person: "Who brings you the most new customers? …You don’t know, right? This shows you — with proof — and rewards them however you choose. It runs itself. First shops run it free. Scan this."  A QR on our phone; the owner is signed up before we leave. ~KES 300–500 to acquire a shop.

Distribution

Every customer is a channel. Density makes it compound.

  • The product distributes the shop. Each customer gets a personal link and QR; their friends are the next customers. That loop is the shop’s distribution — and ours.
  • Density → multi-shop advocates. The same person earning at three shops on one street makes owners recruit each other: "my customers asked if we’re on it."
  • Surfaces we own: ubutangaza.biz (pitch + waitlist), /for-business (scan-to-join QR), the launch video, the article, WhatsApp broadcast.
  • Partners, later: M-Pesa agents, POS resellers, business WhatsApp groups, chamas.

What’s hard to replicate

The code isn’t the moat. What it accumulates is.

1 · A verified referral graph

Every action proof-backed and attributed. After 20 shops we hold the only trustworthy map of who moves customers between businesses on that street. A copycat starts at zero, shop by shop.

2 · Density network effects

Multi-shop advocates make each new shop more valuable to join and each advocate more valuable to keep. Whoever is dense first wins the block.

3 · Trust you can’t fake

An immutable budget and on-chain attestation are a promise no Web2 loyalty tool can make: the shop provably can’t stiff advocates or overrun its costs.

Plus: no custody, no payouts through us — attribution only — so we sit outside VASP scope and pilot faster than licensed fintechs. And the global tools (ReferralCandy, Friendbuy) are e-commerce, USD, card-first: they don’t serve a salon in Kilimani.
